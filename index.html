02 · Read the story

What changed?

Satellite images show the Aral Sea breaking into smaller water bodies and the southern lake losing large areas of open water. NASA’s long-running “World of Change” series documents the retreat from 2000 onward.

Key visual cue: compare the broad dark-blue shapes in 2000 with the much smaller water footprint later in the sequence.

Why did it happen?

NASA Earth Observatory attributes the long-term decline to major diversions of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya for irrigation beginning in the Soviet era. The flow reaching the lake dropped sharply, so the inland sea could no longer maintain its former size.

Important nuance: the North Aral has followed a different trajectory since the Kok-Aral dam was completed in 2005.
03 · Why it matters

Ecology

As the lake shrank and salinity rose, fish populations and the fishing economy were severely disrupted.

Dust & soil

Exposed lakebed can release salty dust, and NASA reports that these particles have affected air quality and agricultural soils.

Climate

NASA notes that losing a huge inland water body changed the local climate, with colder winters and hotter, drier summers.
